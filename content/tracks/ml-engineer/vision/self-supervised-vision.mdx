importantQuestions:
  - question: "What is self-supervised learning for vision?"
    shortAnswer: |
      Train a model to predict some property of the image from another property — without labels. Examples: predict missing patches (MAE), predict augmentation pairs (SimCLR), self-distill (DINO). Produces strong general-purpose features.
    detailed: |
      Self-supervised learning (SSL) uses the data as its own label. Three main families:

      **Contrastive** (SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL, DINO):
      - Two augmented views of the same image should produce similar features.
      - Two views of different images should produce different features.
      - Loss: InfoNCE / contrastive / cosine similarity.

      **Masked modeling** (MAE, BEiT, SimMIM):
      - Mask random patches of an image.
      - Train to reconstruct the masked patches.
      - Like BERT for images.

      **Self-distillation** (DINO, DINOv2):
      - Two views of the image; one passed through a "student" network, one through a "teacher" (EMA of student).
      - Loss: student's predictions should match teacher's, in feature space.
      - No negative pairs needed (unlike SimCLR).

      All produce visual features that transfer to downstream tasks: classification, detection, segmentation, retrieval.

      Why SSL beats supervised pretraining in 2026: the data scale is much larger (1B+ images for DINOv2; ImageNet is 1M). SSL doesn't need labels, so any image is training data.
    pitfalls:
      - "SSL pretraining is expensive: DINOv2 cost ~600,000 GPU-hours to train. Use the released checkpoints; don't train from scratch."
  - question: "What is DINOv2 specifically?"
    shortAnswer: |
      DINOv2 (Meta, 2023) is a ViT pretrained with self-distillation on 142M curated images. Produces strong dense visual features (per-patch embeddings). Has become the default 'feature extractor backbone' for vision in 2026.
    detailed: |
      DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) extends DINO (2021) with:
      - **More data**: 142M curated images (vs. ImageNet's 1M).
      - **Better data curation**: deduplication, balanced sampling, augmented "knowledge distillation" pipeline.
      - **Larger models**: ViT-S/14, ViT-B/14, ViT-L/14, ViT-g/14.
      - **Better features for dense prediction** (segmentation, depth, correspondence).

      Use cases:
      - **Linear probe for classification**: freeze DINOv2, train linear classifier. Strong baseline, often within 2-3 points of full fine-tune.
      - **Fine-tune backbone**: replace ImageNet pretraining with DINOv2.
      - **Dense feature extraction**: per-patch features for downstream tasks (segmentation backbone, dense correspondence).
      - **Few-shot learning**: DINOv2 features + nearest-neighbour classifier on few examples per class.
      - **Out-of-domain transfer**: DINOv2 transfers better than ImageNet to medical, satellite, microscopy.

      Available via HuggingFace (`facebook/dinov2-base`), timm (`vit_base_patch14_dinov2.lvd142m`), or direct from Meta's repo.
    pitfalls:
      - "DINOv2 uses patch size 14 (not 16). Input size must be divisible by 14."
  - question: "What is MAE and where does it fit?"
    shortAnswer: |
      Masked Autoencoder (He et al., 2021): mask 75% of image patches; train a ViT to reconstruct them. Pretrains strong ViTs for transfer; especially good for tasks that benefit from understanding image structure.
    detailed: |
      MAE architecture:

      1. Divide image into 14×14 patches (224×224 image → 16×16 = 256 patches).
      2. Randomly mask 75% of patches.
      3. Pass remaining 25% through ViT encoder.
      4. Decoder reconstructs all patches.
      5. Loss: MSE on masked patches.

      The high masking ratio (75%) is key — at lower ratios the task is too easy and features don't learn meaningfully. 75% is hard enough that the model must learn semantic structure.

      Properties:
      - Strong ViT pretraining without labels.
      - Encoder learns to use sparse information (25% of patches).
      - Transfers well to downstream tasks via fine-tuning.

      MAE itself was superseded somewhat by DINOv2 for general features, but the masked-modeling idea persists in many modern SSL approaches.

      Hybrid: **EVA-02** combines MIM (masked image modeling) with CLIP-style supervision. Strong general-purpose backbone.
    pitfalls:
      - "MAE features perform poorly when used frozen (linear probe). They're optimised for fine-tuning, not for feature extraction directly."
  - question: "How does DINOv2 compare to ImageNet supervised pretraining?"
    shortAnswer: |
      DINOv2 wins on transfer (especially out-of-domain), linear probing, dense prediction, and feature extraction. ImageNet supervised wins on the narrow task of ImageNet classification when fine-tuned. For most production transfer, DINOv2 is the better starting point.
    detailed: |
      Benchmark comparison (ImageNet-1k linear probe):

      | Pretraining | Backbone | Top-1 |
      | ----------- | -------- | ----- |
      | ImageNet supervised | ViT-L | ~85% |
      | CLIP | ViT-L | ~85% |
      | DINOv2 | ViT-L | ~86% |
      | MAE | ViT-L | ~78% (linear probe) but ~86% (fine-tune) |

      For transfer tasks (other datasets, other domains):
      - DINOv2 features transfer dramatically better to fine-grained, satellite, medical.
      - ImageNet supervised features are more "ImageNet-flavoured."

      For dense prediction (segmentation, depth):
      - DINOv2 features are spatially coherent; especially good as a backbone for segmentation / depth estimation.
      - ImageNet supervised features are less dense-aware.

      For 2026 production:
      - General classification: DINOv2 backbone, often beats ImageNet pretraining.
      - Out-of-distribution domain: DINOv2 transfers better.
      - Fine-grained classification: DINOv2 + fine-tune; sometimes domain-specific pretraining matters more.

      ImageNet pretraining still ships in production (lots of legacy models) and isn't bad — DINOv2 is just better for most new projects.
    pitfalls:
      - "DINOv2 was trained on 142M curated images including web scrapes. Not safe for all licensing contexts; check before commercial use."
  - question: "When should you use SSL features vs. fine-tune end-to-end?"
    shortAnswer: |
      Use SSL features (frozen) when: small data (<5k examples), prototyping speed, multi-task. Fine-tune when: > 5k examples, quality matters, can afford the compute.
    detailed: |
      Strategies for using SSL features:

      **Linear probe** (frozen features + linear classifier):
      - Cheap (seconds to train classifier).
      - Strong with DINOv2 — often 95% of fine-tune quality.
      - Best for: <5k examples per class, prototyping.

      **Frozen + small MLP head**:
      - Slightly more capacity than linear; still cheap.
      - Marginal improvement over linear in most cases.

      **Partial fine-tune** (freeze early layers, fine-tune late):
      - Compromise: some capacity, less compute.
      - Useful when full fine-tune overfits.

      **Full fine-tune**:
      - Best quality.
      - Requires more data and compute.
      - Best for: > 5k examples per class, production.

      **LoRA / adapter tuning**:
      - Few trainable parameters; backbone frozen.
      - Useful for very large backbones (DINOv2-g).

      Modern pattern: try linear probe first (free baseline). If quality insufficient, full fine-tune. LoRA for very large backbones.
    pitfalls:
      - "DINOv2 frozen features are a strong baseline. Don't immediately jump to full fine-tune; measure linear probe first."
  - question: "Should I pretrain my own SSL model?"
    shortAnswer: |
      Almost never. SSL pretraining costs 100k-1M GPU-hours. Use released checkpoints (DINOv2, MAE, CLIP). The exception: very domain-specific (medical, satellite) where pretraining on domain data dramatically helps.
    detailed: |
      The economics of SSL pretraining:

      - **DINOv2** training: ~600,000 GPU-hours.
      - **CLIP** training: ~500,000 GPU-hours.
      - **MAE** training: ~200,000 GPU-hours.

      That's millions of dollars of compute. For 99% of teams, use the released checkpoints.

      Exceptions where domain-specific SSL helps:
      - **Medical imaging**: pretrain on PubMed images, X-rays, MRI scans. RadImageNet, MedCLIP, BiomedCLIP.
      - **Satellite / aerial**: pretrain on SpaceNet, xView. SatMAE.
      - **Microscopy**: pretrain on biological data.
      - **Industrial / manufacturing**: pretrain on factory imagery.

      For these, domain-specific pretrained checkpoints often beat general DINOv2 by 5-10 points. Check HuggingFace and academic releases.

      For general / natural images: DINOv2 is the default. Don't pretrain.
    pitfalls:
      - "Self-supervised pretraining on a small in-domain dataset (1k-10k images) usually fails. SSL needs scale to work."

## Why SSL works

The premise: labelled data is expensive; unlabelled data is abundant. Train a model on a pretext task using only the data itself, and the resulting features transfer to downstream tasks.

Three main pretext tasks:

| Family                | Pretext task                                         | Examples           |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| **Contrastive**       | Match augmented views; distinguish from other images | SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL |
| **Masked modeling**   | Reconstruct masked patches                           | MAE, BEiT, SimMIM  |
| **Self-distillation** | Student predicts teacher's features                  | DINO, DINOv2       |

All produce features that are useful for: classification, detection, segmentation, retrieval, dense correspondence, depth estimation.

## DINOv2: the dominant 2026 backbone

DINOv2 (Oquab et al., 2023) trains a ViT with self-distillation:

1. Two augmented views of the same image: x_global, x_local.
2. Pass through student network → features.
3. Pass through teacher network (EMA of student) → features.
4. Loss: cross-entropy between student output and teacher output (treating teacher's softmax distribution as the target).

No labels, no negative pairs. Just "student should match teacher under different views."

Training data: 142M curated images (LVD-142M dataset). Curation is essential — random web scraping is too noisy.

Why DINOv2 matters:

- **Strong dense features**: per-patch ViT outputs are spatially coherent. Good for segmentation, depth, dense matching.
- **Linear probe rivals fine-tuning**: on many benchmarks, frozen DINOv2 + linear classifier is within 2-3 points of full fine-tune.
- **Out-of-domain transfer**: better than ImageNet pretraining for medical, satellite, fine-grained recognition.
- **Multiple sizes**: ViT-S/14 (21M), ViT-B/14 (86M), ViT-L/14 (304M), ViT-g/14 (1.1B).

By 2026, DINOv2 has largely displaced ImageNet-supervised pretraining as the "default backbone" choice.

<AnalogyCallout>
  ImageNet supervised pretraining teaches a model to label 1000 specific classes. DINOv2 teaches a
  model to recognise visual patterns broadly — colours, textures, shapes, parts, objects — without
  committing to any specific taxonomy. The result transfers better, like how a generalist musician
  transitions to new genres more fluently than a virtuoso of one specific genre.
</AnalogyCallout>

## Using DINOv2 in practice

**Linear probe** (frozen features + linear classifier):

```python
import torch
import timm
from sklearn.linear_model import LogisticRegression

model = timm.create_model("vit_base_patch14_dinov2.lvd142m", pretrained=True, num_classes=0)
model.eval()

# Extract features
features = []
labels = []
for x, y in train_loader:
    with torch.no_grad():
        f = model(x.cuda())  # shape: [B, 768] (CLS token feature)
    features.append(f.cpu())
    labels.append(y)
features = torch.cat(features).numpy()
labels = torch.cat(labels).numpy()

# Train linear classifier
clf = LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000, C=1.0)
clf.fit(features, labels)
```

Fast: extract features once (one pass over data); train linear in seconds.

## MAE: the masked-modeling alternative

Masked Autoencoder (He et al., 2021):

1. Image → 16×16 patches.
2. Randomly mask 75% of patches.
3. Encoder (ViT) sees only the 25% unmasked patches.
4. Decoder reconstructs the masked patches.
5. Loss: MSE on masked patches.

Properties:

- **Strong fine-tune backbone**: ViT pretrained with MAE fine-tunes to SOTA on ImageNet.
- **Weak frozen features**: MAE features are optimised for reconstruction, not linear separability. Linear probe accuracy is much lower than DINOv2 / CLIP.
- **Compute-efficient**: encoder only sees 25% of patches → fast training.

Use:

- MAE checkpoint → fine-tune for downstream task. Don't linear-probe.
- DINOv2 checkpoint → linear-probe OR fine-tune (both work).

Modern variants:

- **EVA-02** (BAAI, 2022): combines MIM with CLIP-style supervision. Strong general backbone.
- **BEiT v3** (Microsoft, 2022): unified multimodal pretraining (image + text).

## Comparing pretraining strategies

| Pretraining         | Best for                              | Linear probe (IN-1k) | Fine-tune (IN-1k) |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------- |
| ImageNet supervised | Classification baseline               | ~85% (ViT-L)         | ~85-86%           |
| CLIP                | Vision-language, retrieval, zero-shot | ~85% (ViT-L)         | ~86%              |
| DINOv2              | General features, transfer, dense     | ~86% (ViT-L)         | ~87%              |
| MAE                 | Fine-tune backbone                    | ~78% (ViT-L)         | ~86%              |
| EVA-02              | SOTA general                          | ~88% (ViT-L)         | ~89%              |

Numbers approximate; varies by exact checkpoint. The pattern: DINOv2 and EVA-02 lead for general purpose; CLIP leads for zero-shot and retrieval; MAE for fine-tune (only).

For transfer to non-ImageNet domains (medical, satellite, fine-grained), the gap widens in DINOv2's favour.

## Domain-specific SSL

For specialised domains, domain-pretrained checkpoints often beat general DINOv2:

| Domain                     | Checkpoints                                |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Medical imaging            | MedCLIP, BioMedCLIP, RadImageNet, MoCo-CXR |
| Satellite / aerial         | SatMAE, GeoBert, MERIT                     |
| Microscopy                 | Cellpose, MicroSAM                         |
| Pathology                  | RudolfV, virchowv2, Hibou                  |
| Industrial / manufacturing | Specialised vendor pretrained models       |

These pretrain on millions of in-domain images (medical CTs, satellite imagery, microscopy slides). Transfer to specific downstream tasks dramatically outperforms general pretraining.

When to use:

- Have a domain matching one of the above.
- Limited labelled data.
- Domain-specific visual properties (multi-spectral, non-RGB).

For general / natural images: stick with DINOv2.

## Should you pretrain your own SSL model?

For 99% of teams: no.

Costs:

- DINOv2 training: ~600k GPU-hours.
- CLIP training: ~500k GPU-hours.
- MAE training: ~200k GPU-hours.

That's $1M+ in compute. Use released checkpoints.

Exceptions:

- You have a very specialised domain (medical sub-specialty, niche industrial) with millions of unlabelled images and reason to believe general pretraining doesn't transfer well.
- You have research budget and want to publish.

For these cases: start from a general checkpoint and continue training on your domain data (continued pretraining). Cheaper than from scratch and often as effective.

## Closing for self-supervised vision

Self-supervised pretraining transformed vision in the early 2020s the same way self-supervised pretraining transformed NLP. DINOv2 is the practical default backbone in 2026; CLIP / SigLIP are the dominant for vision-language; domain-specific SSL exists where natural-image pretraining doesn't transfer.

For most production: use the released DINOv2 / CLIP / SigLIP checkpoints. Don't pretrain from scratch. Linear probe as the cheap baseline; fine-tune when you have data and need quality.
